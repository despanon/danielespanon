BAHARIYE BRIDGE

home

The proposal for a new bridge emerges from a critical reading of the river as infrastructure. While rivers have historically acted as connectors, in this case of Istanbul the Golden Horn operates paradoxically as a barrier, physically and perceptually separating two nearby neighborhoods. The project responds to this inversion by introducing a new crossing that not only restores pedestrian continuity, but redefines the river as a shared, active space within the urban network.
Rather than conceiving the intervention as a purely infrastructural element, the project is grounded in a detailed ecological reading of the Bahariye Islands. These islands, historically linked to leisure but now abandoned, are understood as a fragile yet valuable habitat with latent potential. Their current condition (characterized by poor soils, sparse vegetation, and minimal human disturbance) has allowed certain bird species and aquatic fauna to persist, making ecological rehabilitation a central driver of the design.

An elevated pedestrian and cycling catwalk traverses the islands, linking both shores and providing a continuous, immersive route across the river. Importantly, this infrastructure is designed to minimize impact on sensitive areas, allowing users to experience the landscape without disrupting it. In this way, the bridge becomes more than a connection—it acts as a mediating device between urban life and ecological processes.

In their current state, the islands have a clayey and stony bottom, resulting in sparse vegetation; however, bird diversity has been observed in the area. The area appears abandoned and neglected, with uncontrolled entry and exit by boaters navigating the Golden Horn, and it is unable to fully fulfil its ecological functions.

The project concept envisions the Bahariye Islands as habitat islands, highlighting their natural characteristics. Within this scope, the primary goal is to restore the habitat to its former level, improving the living place of the different plants and animals, followed by edge stabilization and soil improvement. For this goal, a step-by-step guide is created for the different processes needed on the transformation of the islands. It guides you through the stages of creating a working and symbiotic environment, explaining the processes needed in the creation of each part of the habitat.

The proposal therefore operates through a dual strategy: reconnecting the city while restoring the environmental integrity of the site. Ultimately, the project repositions the Bahariye Islands as an active interface between city and nature. By integrating mobility, landscape, and habitat restoration, it transforms a neglected and disconnected space into a resilient, multifunctional system that reinforces both urban continuity and environmental performance.
